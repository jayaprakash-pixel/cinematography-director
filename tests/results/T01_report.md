## 9. Decisions explained
1. **The address is hidden for 21 s.** The lock is the subject the viewer trusts. The address is the answer. S01 shows the field but blurs it, so the answer is "in the frame from the start" but cannot be read until 21.0. *Rejected:* address sharp in S01 - it gives the lesson away before the turn.
2. **The lock docks on the line, not on the site (S03).** This is the accuracy point of the Short: TLS protects the transport. The S05 mask drops while the lock stays teal, so the viewer sees "the lock was right, the site was wrong". *Rejected:* a "broken lock" icon for scams - it teaches the opposite (scams have a working lock).
3. **The peak uses a zoom-out, not a push (S06).** "Most" is a quantity; only a widening frame shows quantity. The sync flash of 12 locks on "padlock too" puts the peak on the measured word. *Rejected:* a statistic card - no verified number; and a punch-in shows one site, not many.
4. **Only 3 moves, all zooms.** In 2D a scale is a zoom (no parallax), and all three are "acts of looking". The camera holds during every text window. *Rejected:* parallax push-ins - the diagrams are flat, and parallax adds no information.
5. **One AI insert, not two.** S07 is the one idea that a diagram cannot show as well: "automatic" is physical (a machine that stamps everything). A second insert (for example a person trusting a phone at 13.0) needs a readable phone screen. AI must not generate UI or text, and a screen faced away from the camera teaches nothing. *Rejected:* AI hook shot of a phone browser - the same text problem, and the hook must be the exact lock icon.
6. **The end loops to the start.** The S09 end frame (scale 4.0 on a lock) has the same composition as S01 frame 1. On replay, the first line "That little padlock" plays over the lock the viewer now distrusts.
